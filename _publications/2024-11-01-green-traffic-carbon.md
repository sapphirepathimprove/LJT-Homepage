**Project Title:** Quantitative Model of Green Traffic Carbon Reduction Effects in High-Latitude Frigid Urban Areas

**Evaluation:** Excellent (Top 5% of department)

**Research Background:** High-latitude frigid urban areas face unique challenges in traffic carbon emissions due to extreme cold weather conditions. Cold start emissions account for 40-60% of total emissions in these regions, making green traffic strategies particularly important.

**Methodology:**
- Constructed Bayesian network model integrating meteorological, traffic, and emission data
- Developed prediction model for urban traffic carbon reduction effects under different scenarios
- Proposed seasonal traffic emission management strategies based on multi-factor correlation analysis
- Validated model using real-world traffic and emission data from Changchun city

**Key Findings:**
- Cold start emissions are significantly higher in frigid conditions, requiring targeted management strategies
- Seasonal variations in traffic carbon reduction effects are substantial and need to be considered in policy-making
- Bayesian network approach effectively captures the complex interactions between meteorological and traffic factors

**Significance:** Research results transformed into policy recommendations for Changchun municipal transportation department, contributing to sustainable urban transportation development in cold climate regions.
